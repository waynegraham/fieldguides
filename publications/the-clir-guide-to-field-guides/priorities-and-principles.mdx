## Timely. Credible. Community-based.

These are the general requirements for inclusion in the Field Guides series. Not all Guides will meet every requirement.

- The topic is breaking or rapidly evolving but at its core are issues of evergreen persistence within the information professions
- A collaborative, systemic response is essential
- The topic requires action; descriptive analysis is insufficient
- The emphasis on community and systems is easily intuited
- The problem or challenge affects many people internal and external to memory organizations
- There are significant consequences if the audience remains uninformed
- Guides serve a genuine civic need; the urgency is genuine, not manufactured
- Guides address a knowledge gap that creates real-world risk if left unfilled
- Published information is sufficiently verifiable
- Sources are authoritative and trustworthy
- Publishing won't cause harm
- Privacy and legal obligations are met
- CLIR has the authority or expertise to present the topics credibly, often by alignment with CLIR’s history of projects, programs, and research portfolio
- CLIR resources are available to handle content and publication responsibly

CLIR will consider these questions in making decisions about commissions and selections of works for the series:

- Is the proposed topic relevant to CLIR’s readership?
- Is the topic sufficiently focused to be addressed with nuance in a short work of between 5,000 and 10,000 words?
- Will the content help readers appreciate the significance of contemporary social or technological changes for their work?
- Will the content of the work inform improvements to workflows, practices, or policies in memory organizations?
- Is the content relevant to a variety of types and sizes of organizations and for a range of professional roles and responsibilities?

If interested authors can answer “yes” to these questions, the following additional prompts can help them refine their idea for a potential Field Guide.

1. A paragraph-length summary of what the proposed Field Guide is about and what the primary audience or audiences for the Guide will be.
2. A paragraph-length explanation of why the Guide’s publication is timely and urgent.
3. A short list of other publications that have addressed similar or related topics in the past 10 years, if any.
4. A tentative outline for the Guide and plan for researching, composing, and editing it for public release.
